Collections are selective, not exhaustive. Search results are discovery only; every included source was fetched and read.

## Galaxy S23 Ultra retrospective — 6 September 2026

Eight readable sources from August 2022 through the 1 February 2023 announcement are in `data/models/s23-ultra/snapshots/2026-09-06.json`.

The record covers the 200-megapixel camera, familiar CAD design, reduced display curvature, 5,000 mAh typical battery, Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 for Galaxy, 45-watt charging, 12-megapixel selfie camera and pre-release color names. Samsung Newsroom U.K. launch material is used as official evidence for eventual outcomes; it does not retroactively turn secondary leak chains into independent primary evidence.

The broad hardware predictions were mostly accurate. The leaked Cotton Flower, Botanic Green and Misty Lilac labels did not match Samsung’s final Cream, Green and Lavender names, so that claim remains explicitly disputed. Typical and rated battery capacities are kept distinct.

## Galaxy S27 Family review — 13 September 2026

Thirteen newly collected readable sources and their dates, canonical URLs, provenance and access dates are in `data/snapshots/2026-09-13.json`, which now holds twenty reports and twenty-one sources.

The family record separates four things the baseline held together: the base model's near-unchanged design, a rumored four-model lineup with a new Pro, the Ultra's disputed rear camera treatment, and a possible fifth Edge model. Two of the best-known render sources disagree about the Ultra's rear camera — the OnLeaks CAD keeps a horizontal bar while Ice Universe's sketch and case images show a raised plateau — so both readings are preserved and neither is called settled.

Charging, magnetism and the chipset gained firmer edges: a China 3C listing for 25 W and 45 W, magnetic arrays read into all four CAD sets, and a SemiAnalysis die shot of the Exynos 2700. The base model's 4,900 mAh figure is kept apart from the Ultra's 5,534 mAh rated report, and the earlier rated/typical distinction is unchanged.

Access limitations: the original Android Headlines render and CAD pages could not be read, so their claims are recorded through secondary write-ups and labeled as such. Android Headlines' S27 Plus article exposes no publication date; the earliest Internet Archive capture of 28 August is used as a date bound rather than a stated publication date. Lanzuk's July 2026 silicon-carbon report was read only through a relay. No S27 specification is marked confirmed.

## Galaxy S27 Family review — 20 September 2026

Twenty-four newly collected readable sources and their dates, canonical URLs, provenance and access dates are in `data/snapshots/2026-09-20.json`, which now holds thirty-four reports and forty-five sources.

The display story split into tiers. ETNews reports M16 OLED with Color Filter-on-Encapsulation and Privacy Display for the Pro and Ultra, an M14 step for the base and Plus, and a 6.27 / 6.66 / 6.47 / 6.89-inch set of panel sizes. The same sourcing now narrows Privacy Display to two models, reversing a July account that gave it to the whole family, so both readings are kept; a mid-September round-up still repeats the all-models version. PhoneArena's round-up adds the opposite pressure on the base model — BOE panels under consideration, a possible M13 retention that would contradict the M14 step, and third-party display driver ICs — recorded as a live disagreement rather than a decision.

Charging and battery moved onto firmer ground. China's 3C listings put the Pro and Ultra at 60 W (20 V/3 A), with battery filings reported at 5,087 mAh rated for the Pro and 5,534 mAh rated for the Ultra, which outlets convert to roughly 5,200 and 5,700 mAh advertised. The earlier July reading of 5,534 mAh for the Ultra is retained and now carries the certification coverage as supporting evidence alongside its original relay. A separate reading of the same filings puts the base and Plus cells at 4,300 and 4,900 mAh, which contradicts the archive's August 4,900 mAh base figure; both are preserved.

The camera whiplash resolved toward continuity. The late-August 4x 1/1.9-inch telephoto proposal is recorded as its own report, and Ice Universe's 14 September rejection of it is recorded separately, with two further outlets attributing the retained 50-megapixel Sony 5x module to Roland Quandt. The Ultra's tipped video and colour rework, the reportedly rejected 7,000 mAh S Pen-less prototype, the snapdragon-tier and LPDDR5X/LPDDR6 splits, the January-February 2027 launch window and the expected price ladder each stand as distinct reports.

Access limitations: the Chinese 3C registry entries were read only through outlet coverage, and the model-number mapping to the Pro and Ultra is those outlets' inference. The Ice Universe, Roland Quandt, phonefuturist, GalaxyFlash2025 and WalleGalaxy posts were read only through secondary attributions, so the leaker index records them without treating repetition as verification. Imaging Resource's camera write-up returned an unsolvable captcha and is excluded. Android Headlines pages remain unreadable. No S27 specification is marked confirmed.

## Galaxy S27 Family review — 27 September 2026

Thirteen newly collected readable sources and their dates, canonical URLs, provenance and access dates are in `data/snapshots/2026-09-27.json`, which now holds thirty-eight reports and fifty-eight sources.

The memory and storage picture firmed up without gaining independence. SammyGuru, Android Authority, GSMArena, Sammy Fans, Gagadget and TelecomTalk all published the same ladder between 21 and 24 September — a 12 GB floor, 256 GB at entry, a 1 TB Pro tier held at 12 GB, and a single 16 GB with 1 TB option on the Ultra. Three of them trace to the same Korean supply-chain talk attributed to the Naver blogger Lanzuk, and one is a relay relaying him, so the archive keeps them as one report chain rather than six witnesses. The LPDDR6 and UFS 5.1 expectation for the Pro and Ultra arrived with a tier caveat in GSMArena's and Gagadget's reading: the new standards may reach only the higher storage tiers, and the base and Plus are not expected to move to them at all.

Two smaller threads arrived with it. Sammy Fans reported, via SammyGuru's reading of trade data, that the programme's internal labels moved from NM1–NM4 under the New/Next Miracle codename to Trinity1–Trinity4. Tech Advisor's 25 September round-up reports the variable-aperture comeback abandoned on cost and repeats the Pro and Ultra dimensions from the August OnLeaks CAD set; that page also argues against itself, still listing a possible variable-aperture comeback in its own summary bullets, which is preserved as written rather than tidied away.

Privacy Display produced the week's clearest single-source claim. Ice Universe posted on 24 September that the Ultra will adopt Xiaomi's split-screen privacy behaviour — one app protected in a two-app view, with the protected region following the divider as it moves — a day after the Xiaomi 18 Pro shipped the equivalent in China. Four outlets relayed it within two days, all pointing at the same post, so it is recorded as one report at low confidence and kept apart from the older question of which models get Privacy Display hardware at all.

One contradiction was added rather than resolved: TelecomTalk's 23 September round-up says the new Pro replaces the Edge line, against the 2 September accounts that put an S27 Edge in development alongside it.

Access limitations: Android Headlines remained unreadable, failing to render even through the browser tier, and Forbes' 21 September battery-filing piece returned a browser launch failure, so neither is cited. The Sunday Guardian's 24 September Exynos mainboard aggregation names no source, document or filing and is carried as context only. A Nokia Power User specification table credited to an X account printed only as "@Batman" was excluded as unverifiable rather than recorded. Every named tipster post, including Ice Universe's split-screen post and Lanzuk's configuration list, was read through secondary coverage. No S27 specification is marked confirmed.

## Galaxy S27 Family review — 4 October 2026

Twenty-three newly collected readable sources and their dates, canonical URLs, provenance and access dates are in `data/snapshots/2026-10-04.json`, which now holds forty-seven reports and eighty-one sources.

The week turned the programme physical. The Elec reported that Samsung began full-scale front-end wafer production of the Exynos 2700 in early October at a volume more than 10% above the Exynos 2600's, with the base S27, S27 Plus and the new S27 Pro as its intended homes and the Ultra still undecided; GSMArena, SamMobile, Android Headlines and Sammy Fans relayed it within a day. A separate report says an Exynos-powered Ultra prototype is now in testing, which the archive keeps as one tipster chain rather than as corroboration of the production story. Sammy Fans also relayed a ZDNet report that some components entered production in September, that Samsung Display is starting OLED panel output against roughly 47–48 million panels, and that some Galaxy S26 parts will be reused as memory prices climb.

The camera picture narrowed. Ice Universe restated on 29 September that the Ultra drops its 10-megapixel 3x telephoto and keeps only the 50-megapixel 5x module, unchanged at 1/2.52-inch and f/2.9, and added a 200-megapixel ISOCELL HP6 main sensor with warmer colour and better highlight retention; 9to5Google, Android Authority, SammyGuru and Gagadget relayed it. The three-camera geometry is separately readable in OnLeaks' 28 September render, but the two best-known render accounts now openly disagree about where inside the pill the telephoto sits, so that argument is filed as its own disputed report rather than folded into the removal. Gagadget describes the earlier larger 4x sensor option as dead, which the archive records against the existing proposal rather than deleting it.

Two smaller threads arrived. Ice Universe put the Ultra at 218 g and 7.9 mm, four grams heavier than the S26 Ultra at the same thickness, and screen-protector listings for all four models were read as showing near-unchanged fronts and sizes consistent with the earlier ETNews panel figures. A Korean post attributed to the Naver leaker Lanzuk, also reported as yeux1122, said the whole S27 line rises in price with steeper steps between storage tiers — against the earlier expectation that only the Ultra would move.

Access limitations: The Elec was read in full on its English edition, but every named tipster post — Ice Universe's camera, weight and screen-protector material, OnLeaks' render, kro's Exynos and telephoto claims and Lanzuk's price post — was read only through secondary coverage, and the photo-heavy Android Headlines pages came back as chrome on some fetches, so their summaries and body text carry the claims. No S27 specification is marked confirmed.

## Galaxy S27 Family review — 11 October 2026

Fourteen newly collected readable sources and their dates, canonical URLs, provenance and access dates are in `data/snapshots/2026-10-11.json`, which now holds fifty reports and ninety-five sources.

The colour story broke first. Ice Universe posted on 5 October that the Ultra's base colours are Black, Blue, Light Pink and White and added a special Violet the same day; Android Authority, 9to5Google, SamMobile and Sammy Fans relayed it within a day, all pointing at that single post. Light Pink would take the place of the S26 Ultra's Cobalt Violet, and 9to5Google reads the list as leaving purple out of the Ultra for the first time since 2023 while Android Authority's same-day update keeps a Violet as a special edition, so both readings sit side by side rather than one being picked.

Charging produced the week's narrowest claim. WalleGalaxy's report, read through Android Authority and repeated by Geeky Gadgets on 9 October, has the Ultra holding its 60 W ceiling while sustaining roughly 55 W to 56 W for longer, worth about 2% to 3% in charge time on a larger 5,700 mAh cell. Tech Advisor's 8 October update carries the same figures but credits phonefuturist and Ice Universe, so the archive records the numbers and the disagreement about who produced them.

Price and display arrived together from one @phonefuturist post on 8 October: $899, $1,099, $1,299 and $1,399 for the United States, with global and Korean prices a tier higher at $999, $1,199, $1,399 and $1,499 to $1,549, plus an OLED split that gives the base and Plus an M14+ panel and the Pro and Ultra the M16 panel with Privacy Display. 9to5Google, Sammy Fans, Trusted Reviews and PhoneArena relayed it. The ladder holds the US base price and puts the Ultra below the late-August expectation, which contradicts the 30 September Korean reading of a rise across every tier; both are kept.

The chipset question reversed again on 9 October. Reporting attributed to the Naver blogger Lanzuk says Samsung has no plans for an Exynos Ultra in any market, keeping Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 for Galaxy worldwide in the Ultra while the base, Plus and Pro take the Exynos 2700 across most of Europe, Asia and Africa and Snapdragon covers North America and China. SammyGuru, Sammy Fans, Nokia Power User and Samsung Magazine relayed it, all naming the same account. It sits against the 1 and 2 October prototype and production reports, so it is filed as its own disputed report that contradicts them instead of superseding them: nothing in it disputes the Exynos 2700 order, only which models receive the chip.

Access limitations: no named tipster post was read directly — Ice Universe's colour post, WalleGalaxy's charging claim, phonefuturist's price and display post and Lanzuk's chipset allocation were all read through relays. Android Headlines could not be opened in any tier in this run, failing on its pricing, chipset and vapour-chamber pages, so nothing from Android Headlines is cited and its "No Exynos 2700 for the Galaxy S27 Ultra After All" piece is represented only by the relays that name the same source. Tech Advisor and SamMobile are continuously updated pages, so the dates recorded here come from their own update timestamps rather than an article date. No S27 specification is marked confirmed.

## Galaxy S27 Family review — 18 October 2026

Twenty-four newly collected readable sources and their dates, canonical URLs, provenance and access dates are in `data/snapshots/2026-10-18.json`, which now holds fifty-seven reports and one hundred and nineteen sources.

Silicon dominated the week. Seoul Economic Daily reported on 16 October that Samsung's MX division found the Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 exceeding thermal thresholds and throttling in Galaxy S27 Ultra verification, asked Qualcomm to fix it by mid-October, and would consider the Exynos 2700 if it did not. 9to5Google, Android Authority and SamMobile relayed it; Qualcomm called the account inaccurate. The archive records the report and the denial as one disputed claim rather than treating the company statement as a refutation of the tests, and notes that Gizmochina found the public denial naming the standard 8 Elite Gen 6 rather than the Extreme variant at issue. A 17 October relay says the Ultra decision remains open, against the 9 October reading that Samsung had no Exynos Ultra plans.

The regulatory thread produced the week's clearest hardware downgrade. Chinese MIIT entries read through Abhishek Yadav list UWB on the Galaxy S27 Pro and S27 Ultra but not on the base or Plus; Android Authority, SamMobile and GSMArena relayed it on 16 October. The base model has never carried UWB, so the Plus is the real change, and SamMobile notes the filings may be preliminary. The leaker index moves Yadav from low to medium on the strength of a second regulatory-filing claim, with the relay and mapping limits stated in the entry.

Camera tiering sharpened on 14 October. GalaxyClub reported, without citing a source, that the S27 and S27+ keep the 12MP autofocus selfie camera while the Pro and Ultra get a new 16MP sensor that may be square. This narrows the archive's July 16MP claim rather than contradicting it, since that claim concerned the Ultra.

Two display claims stayed single-source: @WalleGalaxy's DC dimming for the Pro and Ultra, expected in a hybrid scheme that keeps PWM at low brightness, and @Sawyergalox's second-generation Privacy Display with better angles and clarity. PhoneArena's 10 October piece supplies context on the S26 Ultra's Privacy Display complaints but no independent confirmation of the fix.

Design measurements arrived with a contradiction inside them. Ice Universe gave 1.28 mm bezels and a 4.69 mm camera bump on 15 October. GSMArena compared them with a 1.15 mm S26 Ultra bezel and read the change as thicker; SamMobile and Gizmochina used 1.32 mm and read it as slimmer. Sammy Fans attributed a separate set — 1.21 mm and 4.64 mm — to OnLeaks. The archive therefore records the figures and the conflicting baselines rather than a direction.

A 13 October price roadmap attributed to Anthony (@TheGalox_) put the four models at $1,099, $1,199, $1,399 and $1,499, against the 8 October @phonefuturist ladder of $899 to $1,399. Sammy Fans' prose once gives the Pro as $1,199 while its own list says $1,399. Both ladders are kept as disputed expectations; the shared direction is higher prices on memory costs.

Access limitations: Android Headlines could not be opened in any tier, including its S27, S27+ and S27 Pro preview pages, so nothing from Android Headlines is cited and its 16 October updates are represented only through relays. Every named tipster post — Ice Universe's measurements and case video, WalleGalaxy's DC dimming, SawyerGalox's Privacy Display v2, Abhishek Yadav's MIIT reading and TheGalox's price list — was read through secondary coverage. Megamobilecontent's DC dimming page and tbreak's case article were blocked or refused and are not cited. No S27 specification is marked confirmed.

## Galaxy S27 Family review — 25 October 2026

Eleven newly collected readable sources and their dates, canonical URLs, provenance and access dates are in `data/snapshots/2026-10-25.json`, which now holds sixty-three reports and one hundred and thirty sources.

The week's story was subtraction. On 20 October Sammy Fans and NokiaPowerUser both reported that the Galaxy S27 and S27+ fall back to an 8 GB tier, with the Pro at 12 GB and the Ultra at 12 GB or 16 GB, blaming DRAM prices. That contradicts the 21 September reading of twelve gigabytes across the family, and since neither piece names an account, a document or a supply-chain outlet, the archive keeps both ladders and files the new one as disputed rather than as a replacement. Lanzuk's 19 October post supplies the same pressure from the other side: the launch schedule and major components are finalised, but whether the Pro and Ultra get LPDDR6 and UFS 5.0 is still open on cost.

The same day produced a second subtraction. SammyGuru published screen protectors for only the S27, S27+ and S27 Ultra, at 6.27, 6.66 and 6.89 inches, with no S27 Pro cut. Sammy Fans and Android Authority both noted the gap and both cautioned that an absent protector is not a cancelled model; BigGo Finance went further on 21 October and read it as a possible three-model lineup, citing the S26 Pro and S26 Edge as precedents. Because the archive's late-September protector reading put four fronts close to the S26, the two rounds genuinely disagree, and the newer one is recorded as a contradiction.

Design moved in the opposite direction. Ice Universe's 18 October side-by-side case photograph, relayed by Sammy Fans and GSMArena, shows the S27 and S27+ keeping vertical camera cutouts while the Pro and Ultra take rectangular ones spanning nearly the full width. That is the best artifact yet for the split, and it favours the horizontal-bar reading over the disputed plateau reading, though it is still one account's photo of third-party tooling.

Battery gained its firmest evidence in months, in two steps. Safety Korea listed the two cell units, EB-BS958DBY and EB-BS957CBY, made by Zhuhai CosMX Power Jinwan, without readable capacities on 22 October. Two days later TÜV Rheinland listed the same model numbers with figures: 5,534 mAh rated and 5,700 mAh typical for the Ultra, 5,087 mAh rated and 5,240 mAh typical for the Pro. The rated numbers match the September 3C filings, which is what makes the typical pair worth keeping, and the Pro's 5,240 mAh sits 40 mAh above the 5,200 mAh earlier coverage assumed. Rated and typical stay separate units.

Access limitations: Forbes' 20 October piece on the case designs and the launch date returned an unsolvable captcha and is not cited. Every named tipster post — Ice Universe's 18 October image, Lanzuk's 19 October schedule — was read only through relays, and the TÜV and Safety Korea entries were read through Sammy Fans rather than at the registries. The Samsung Global Newsroom front page carried no S27 item through 23 October, so nothing here is official and no S27 specification is marked confirmed.

## Integrity rule

A retrospective collection date is not a claim that the archive observed a report before launch. `firstObservedAt` records when this archive collected the material; `sourceDate` preserves when the source published it. Existing snapshots remain immutable after publication.
